The idea, in a sentence

Instead of one model taking a wild guess at the market, TradingAgents runs a small team of AI agents that behave like a real investment desk. Each one has a job. They argue it out. A portfolio manager makes the final call.

Who sits on the desk

  • News agent — reads the headlines and earnings for each stock and works out what actually matters.
  • Macro agent — watches rates, inflation and the broad market. The weather overhead.
  • Sentiment agent — reads the mood. Is the crowd greedy, scared, or half asleep.
  • The researchers — a bull and a bear take opposite sides and genuinely argue each name.
  • The trader — turns that argument into a proposed move.
  • The risk team — stress-tests the move before it is allowed anywhere near the book.
  • The portfolio manager — weighs the lot and sets the final position.

Why a team beats one model

Ask a single model "should I buy this?" and you usually get a confident, shallow yes. Force a bull and a bear to fight over it, then make a risk team sign off, and the weak points that one quick answer would gloss over come straight to the surface. You end up with a decision that shows its working, not a hunch dressed up as certainty.

How a call actually gets made

Every position starts as a five-step relay. Nothing skips a stage, and each one can send the decision back down the line.

  1. 01
    Gather. The analyst agents pull the day's prices, fundamentals, recent news and the macro backdrop for each name on the watchlist.
  2. 02
    Debate. A bull and a bear researcher argue the case back and forth, for as many rounds as it takes to stop repeating themselves.
  3. 03
    Propose. The trader takes the stronger side of that debate and writes a concrete move: buy, hold or sell, and how much.
  4. 04
    Stress-test. The risk team attacks the proposal on a risk-first basis, and can send it back if it runs too hot for the fund's mandate.
  5. 05
    Commit. The portfolio manager makes the final call, sizes the position, and writes the one-line reason you read on the Commentary page.

Under the hood

The heavier version: TradingAgents is built on LangGraph, which wires the agents into a graph they pass their findings through. It pulls live prices and fundamentals, recent news and macro data, runs each agent as its own language-model call, and lets the researcher and risk stages run several rounds of back-and-forth before the manager commits. It is open research from Tauric Research, so anyone can check exactly how it reaches a call: read the paper on arXiv or the code on GitHub.

Where LiquidAssets comes in

This is where the research meets a real hand on the tiller. LiquidAssets is run by a finance and computer science student who has traded the US and New Zealand markets for years, and who took TradingAgents and pivoted it into three funds at different risk levels — Whitewater, Tidewater and Stillwater — so the same engine can suit a bold appetite or a cautious one. The academic work does the reasoning. The judgement about how to point it, and at what risk, is ours.
